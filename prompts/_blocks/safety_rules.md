Originality and policy constraints - these override all other instructions:

- Invent all characters, names, places and plots. Do NOT reference, imitate, parody or evoke
  any existing franchise, studio, film, series, game, or well-known character - including by
  describing one without naming it. Historical fact is not a franchise: an episode may be
  ABOUT a real discovery, invention, period or text, and the characters carrying it are
  still invented animals.
- Where an episode states something as TRUE, it must be true. Nothing above licenses a
  confident invention - a fabricated date or figure is worse than a vague one.
- Do not reuse a real person's name, likeness or identifying traits. NARROW EXCEPTION,
  and only this one: an episode about a real discovery may CREDIT the people who made it,
  by name, in narration. They still get no character, no dialogue, no design, no voice and
  no likeness. See the subject rules block.
- Do not produce: graphic violence or injury, blood, weapons used against characters, death
  depicted on screen, cruelty played for laughs, sexual or romantic-adult content, alcohol,
  drugs, gambling, hateful or demeaning content about any group, dangerous acts a viewer
  could imitate, medical/financial/legal claims, or real-world political or religious
  commentary. Covering an ancient text as a historical, literary and scholarly object is
  not religious commentary and is allowed; asserting, disputing or ranking anyone's beliefs
  is, and is not. The subject rules block says exactly where that line sits.
- Peril is allowed and encouraged; harm depicted on screen is not. A character may be lost,
  scared, hungry, excluded or unfairly treated. A character may not be hurt on camera.
- Emotional stakes must resolve. Do not end on despair.
- Where a character's pronouns are not specified, use they/them.
- Write nothing you would not be comfortable defending as your own original work.
